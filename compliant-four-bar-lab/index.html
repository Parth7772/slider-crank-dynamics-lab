    Motor Torque, Power & Energy

    The motor must accelerate the links and bend the flexures. Near resonance the flexures return the energy the links need, so the motor works far less than for a rigid pin-jointed linkage.

    Problem Statement & Notation

    Setting. A planar four-bar chain made in one piece. The frame link has length ; the input link (), coupler () and output link () are rigid bars of width and thickness . The joints are flexure hinges of length , thickness and width , unstressed in the as-made position . A payload sits at point of the coupler. A motor drives the input link through .

    Find the motion of every link, the stiffness of the flexures, the motor torque and power, the natural frequency and vibration response, the joint forces, the hinge stresses and their fatigue safety, and the drive frequency that needs the least motor torque.

    Coordinates. Angles are measured counter-clockwise from the frame line ; is the drive phase. Torque is positive counter-clockwise on the input link.

    Data (live).

    frame, input, coupler and output link lengths
    link angles; as-made input angle
    kinematic coefficients and their derivatives
    transmission angle
    elastic modulus, hinge second moment of area, hinge stiffness
    rotation of the flexure at joint and
    elastic energy, equivalent stiffness and inertia at the input
    motor torque and its inertia, spring and damping parts
    natural frequency, damping ratio, frequency ratio
    alternating and mean stress, fatigue and yield safety factors

    Assumptions.

    • Planar motion in a horizontal plane (gravity does not act in the plane).
    • Rigid links; flexures modelled as small-length flexural pivots ( link length), linear elastic.
    • Prescribed sinusoidal input motion; damping proportional to hinge stiffness.
    • Hinge stress includes the joint force conservatively (as both bending and direct stress).

    Numerics. 720 positions per drive cycle (0.5°), closed-form kinematics, RK4 time integration, quadrature for the free-oscillation period.
